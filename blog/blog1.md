# Why learning data science in social and interdisciplinary contexts matters
Taking data points on individuals is a tricky subject in the world of data science. In the world of sports analytics it is fairly simple to take stats on the field and court. Seeing how players react to a curveball
while they are down 0-2 in the count is an easy data point to track. Outside of that it gets a bit trickier. Taking data on athletes sleep schedules, their diet, have they been drinking more, and other factors bleed into a personal point where some people are not comfortable sharing. This also comes into play with traditional data science, in social media and advertising. When taking these data points from individuals we need to realize that this data is important and should not always be shared. An easy example is with applying to jobs, where they can get your SSN for a background check. These obviously should not be shared so your identity is not stolen. Then there is data on google and apple maps, where your location is tracked. If companies are sharing this data, obviously others having your location at all times is bad. 

In this course I am hoping how to better understand how data can be used to show others the best outcomes through data. Especially with sports in general. Seeing what athletes play better on what day, against what teams, and how to beat defenses through numbers is very inspiring for me.
